Section 01

Problem or Idea

Flooding is a recurring hazard across Philippine communities during periods of heavy rainfall and typhoons. Floodwaters rise rapidly, making roads impassable and endangering residents before authorities can establish complete situational awareness.

Manual Observation Lag

Disaster monitoring relies on physical ocular inspections and disparate resident phone calls, leaving substantial visibility gaps when multiple areas submerge simultaneously.

Absence of Visual Context

Isolated water probes signal contact moisture but cannot verify road passability, vehicle clearance, surface accumulation velocity, or physical debris risks.

Delayed Emergency Action

Without centralized, technology-assisted verification, local response units risk dispatching rescue assets reactively rather than initiating preemptive road closures.

Section 02

Brief Description of Proposed Solution

AGOS AI is an AI-assisted flood detection and early-warning system designed to help local disaster response authorities monitor flood-prone areas through computer vision and centralized alerting.

Core Solution Framework

01
Stationary Camera Emulation: A laptop webcam acts as a proof-of-concept stationary monitoring camera overlooking a road or drainage benchmark.
02
Computer Vision Inference: Model identifies visible water surfaces and calculates accumulation against reference markers in a calibrated scene.
03
Automated Risk Classification: Detected conditions are measured against predefined thresholds: Normal, Watch, Warning, or Critical.
04
Authority Decision Dashboard: Real-time risk data and photographic evidence are relayed to an authenticated command interface.

System Distinction

Evidence-Backed Alerting

Rather than sending raw sensor figures, alerts deliver timestamped visual snapshots, allowing responders to verify conditions before field deployment.

End-to-End Workflow

Connects edge detection directly to authority-facing acknowledgment workflows, closing the loop between detection and emergency decision-making.

Section 03

General & Specific Objectives

Establishing technical and operational goals to develop, calibrate, and evaluate an automated early-warning prototype.

General Objective

To develop an AI-assisted flood monitoring and early-warning prototype that provides disaster response authorities with timely visual information, preliminary flood-risk assessments, and centralized alerts to support community safety and emergency decision-making.

1. Computer Vision Pipeline

Develop a vision pipeline capable of detecting surface water presence and pooling boundaries within camera video feeds.

2. Benchmark Estimation

Implement an edge method for tracking observed flood level variations against fixed reference marks within a calibrated scene.

3. Configurable Risk Engine

Establish dynamic threshold rules to automatically calculate risk tiers and trigger appropriate alert states.

4. Authority Web Platform

Develop an authenticated dashboard for viewing monitoring feeds, reviewing visual evidence, and recording response actions.

5. Notification Mechanism

Implement alert logging, grouping, and notification dispatch to eliminate duplicate alert fatigue among disaster personnel.

6. Empirical Evaluation

Evaluate detection accuracy, alert delivery latency, and environmental resilience under varied simulated lighting and water states.

Section 04

Target Users & Beneficiaries

Designed specifically for operational command staff requiring verified data prior to dispatching personnel into hazard zones.

Primary Stakeholders

Local Disaster Risk Reduction & Management Offices (LDRRMOs)

Municipal and city emergency personnel managing emergency operations centers and coordinating multi-zone resource deployment.

Barangay DRRM Committees (BDRRMC)

Local village officials responsible for primary community advisories, neighborhood gate barriers, and initial street closures.

Road & Drainage Maintenance Crews

Public works crews monitoring critical catch basins, culverts, and low-lying road corridors for clearing operations.

Indirect Beneficiaries

Residents of Flood-Prone Communities

Protected from dangerous road conditions through timely, validated public safety advisories and preemptive municipal actions.

Emergency First Responders

Ambulances and rescue units receive situational route awareness, preventing emergency vehicles from becoming submerged or stranded.

Section 05

Proposed Key Features

Core technical capabilities supporting real-time visual observation, automated risk staging, and authority workflow management.

Central Command Dashboard

Consolidated interface displaying registered camera monitoring nodes, active risk classifications, and latest frame captures.

Benchmark Risk Engine

Automated classification engine tracking surface water elevation against reference levels into four calibrated operational tiers.

Incident Evidence Logging

Each alert captures timestamped visual frames, reference water elevation metrics, and audit entries for human verification.

Calibrated Risk Classification Matrix

The system maps observed camera measurements directly to predefined authority actions:

Status Observed Condition System Action
NORMAL No significant pooling detected Routine background monitoring
WATCH Visible water accumulation or increasing trend Dashboard status flag updated
WARNING Water reaches predefined reference benchmark Dispatches high-priority alert to authorities
CRITICAL Water crosses configured danger threshold Triggers urgent notification for immediate action
Simulate Risk Stage: NORMAL
0% (Dry) 35% (Watch) 65% (Warning) 100% (Critical)
Section 06

Proposed Technologies & Tools

A modular technology stack structured for low-barrier prototyping and scalable migration to permanent field hardware.

AI & Computer Vision

Python pipeline utilizing OpenCV and lightweight convolutional/YOLO models for water region segmentation and reference benchmark tracking.

Python 3.11 OpenCV YOLO Architecture

Backend & APIs

Java with Spring Boot (or FastAPI) for role-based authentication, alert queue processing, telemetry ingestion, and REST endpoints.

Spring Boot FastAPI REST API

Database & Dashboard

Relational database for storing incident audit logs and camera nodes, paired with an HTML5/CSS/JavaScript responsive authority dashboard.

MySQL HTML5 / CSS Modern JS

Prototype Hardware Specification

Initial demonstration is powered entirely by a standard laptop with an integrated webcam simulating a stationary roadside monitoring node. Demonstrations can be performed using miniature roadway models, controlled water containers, or pre-recorded flood footage. Optional extensions (ESP32 microcontrollers with water-depth sensors) can be linked modularly without altering the core software architecture.
